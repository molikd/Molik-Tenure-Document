\section{Supporting the scholarly communication lifecycle}
My work connects library services with computational methods so that people can create, publish, share, preserve, find and reuse knowledge. Libraries participate directly in the scholarly communication process: they provide the guidance, infrastructure and services that carry research from an author's draft to a preserved, reusable public record. Scholarly Communication allows libraries to own and understand this process. As Department Head of the Open Publishing Exchange (OPE) and as Library Lead Research Officer, I work across the scholarly communication lifecycle: publishing, digital preservation, access and data reuse, and argueably most importantly in this leadership role, on the stratagey to develop these library competencies as well as the relationships and networks that keep it going. My applied information science research studies the same questions: how to keep records of where information came from, how to connect research tools, and how to make resources reusable. Genomics is one setting for that research, alongside the library services and reporting tools described below. This years portfolio covers February 25, 2025 through September 15, 2026, with the 2026 work given the area for review.

\subsection{Sustainability: my goal, the goal, and the ethical framework: In short, an obession}
Sustainability is both my major goal and a substantial part of my ethical framework for librarianship. Library services should outlive the people who launch them, and they should continue to serve scholars and students without depending on any one person's heroic effort. The June 2026 OPE landscape and executive summary that I wrote defines sustainability in four dimensions, and I use those dimensions throughout this portfolio, as well in the decisions I make and leadership I provide:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Financial sustainability:} services have clear funding paths through student fees, endowments, grants, memoranda of understanding (MOUs), cost recovery and university investment.
\item \textbf{Operational sustainability:} services have defined workflows, staffing, scope and expectations, so growth does not rely on unsupported individual effort.
\item \textbf{Technical sustainability:} platform decisions support preservation, interoperability, accessibility, metadata quality and realistic maintenance. Infrastructure should be supportable, maintainable and modular. 
\item \textbf{Intellectual sustainability:}  New services should be documented well, training should be provided, and processes recorded. Additionally, As OPE builds a research program that studies scholarly communication, open infrastructure, data publishing, open educational resources (OER) and evidence of impact.
\end{itemize}
I use these dimensions to explain purpose and significance. In such a intelectual framework, a proposed funding path is a goal, a workflow design is an approach, and a service running in production is an outcome; I keep those distinctions visible rather than treating ``sustainable'' as self-explanatory or largely only fiscally based.

\subsection{Network building as a supporting thread}
Building networks and relationships is the second thread that connects the work. Library services, grant proposals, shared standards and research communities all depend on relationships: mentoring a colleague through a funding application, coordinating a campus partnership, or sustaining an external community such as i5k or AgBioData. Sustainability remains the goal and the ethical framework; network building is how the work gets done and often enough, how it lasts.
