\section{Supporting the scholarly communication lifecycle}
My work connects library services with computational methods so that people can create, publish, share, preserve, find, and reuse knowledge. In practice, this work largely takes the form of leading a team and services responsible for both the technical and operational infrastructure of scholarly communication, supported by a research program focused on improving the effectiveness of those systems. Libraries participate directly in the scholarly communication process: libraries provide the guidance, infrastructure, and services that carry research from an author’s draft to a discoverable and reusable public record. Scholarly communication therefore gives libraries both responsibility for and insight into the a core process of the research enterprise.

As Department Head of the Open Publishing Exchange (OPE), a Librarian, and Library Lead Research Officer, I work across the scholarly communication lifecycle, including publishing, digital preservation, access, and data reuse. Arguably most important in this leadership role, however, is developing the strategy, organizational competencies, relationships, and professional networks that allow the Libraries to sustain and expand this work.

My applied information science research addresses many of the same questions: how to preserve records of where information came from, how to connect research tools and systems, and how to make scholarly resources more reusable. Genomics, where it appears in my research, is just one setting for that research, alongside the library services, research infrastructure, and reporting tools described below.

This portfolio covers February 25, 2025 through September 15, 2026, with particular emphasis on work completed during 2026.

\subsection{Sustainability: my goal, the goal, and the ethical framework: In short, an obsession}

Sustainability is both a major goal of my work and a substantial part of my ethical framework for librarianship. Library services should outlive the people who launch them, and they should continue to serve scholars and students without depending on any one person's heroic effort. The June 2026 OPE landscape and executive summary that I wrote defines sustainability across four dimensions. I use these dimensions throughout this portfolio, as well as in the decisions I make and the leadership I provide:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Financial sustainability:} Services have clear and realistic funding paths through student fees, endowments, grants, memoranda of understanding (MOUs), cost recovery, university investment, or an appropriate combination of these mechanisms.

\item \textbf{Operational sustainability:} Services have defined and documented workflows, staffing, scope, responsibilities, and expectations so that growth does not depend on unsupported individual effort or institutional memory held by a single person.

\item \textbf{Technical sustainability:} Platform and infrastructure decisions support preservation, interoperability, accessibility, metadata quality, and realistic long-term maintenance. Systems should be supportable, maintainable, and modular enough to evolve as technologies and institutional needs change.

\item \textbf{Intellectual sustainability:} Services should retain and develop the knowledge necessary to operate, evaluate, and improve them. New services should be well documented, training should be provided, and processes and decisions should be recorded so that expertise can be transferred rather than remaining with individuals. OPE's developing research program extends this principle by studying scholarly communication, open infrastructure, data publishing, open educational resources (OER), and evidence of impact so that future service decisions can be informed by evidence rather than convention alone.
\end{itemize}

I use these dimensions not only as administrative criteria, but also to explain purpose, significance, and progress in my daily work, but also here in my tenure and evaluation materials. Within this intellectual framework, a proposed funding path is a goal, a workflow design is an approach, and a service operating in production is an outcome. I keep those distinctions visible throughout this portfolio rather than treating ``sustainable'' as self-explanatory or reducing sustainability primarily to fiscal stability.

\subsection{Network building as a supporting thread}
Building networks and relationships is the second thread that connects the work. Library services, grant proposals, shared standards and research communities all depend on relationships: mentoring a colleague through a funding application, coordinating a campus partnership, or sustaining an external community such as i5k or AgBioData. Sustainability remains the goal and the ethical framework; network building is so often how the work gets done and how it lasts.
