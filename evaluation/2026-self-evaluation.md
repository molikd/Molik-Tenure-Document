## Overview

My central goal as Department Head of the Open Publishing Exchange (OPE) is to build scholarly communication services that the Libraries can sustain. Scholarly communication encompasses how research is created, published, shared, and preserved. The Libraries participate in that process by providing guidance, infrastructure, and services that help researchers and students make their work available and usable. My responsibility is to help those services develop with clear purposes, realistic commitments, and people prepared to carry them forward.

Sustainability is also a substantial part of my ethical approach to librarianship. When the Libraries offer a publishing or data service, we create expectations about access, stewardship, and continuity. I want our commitments to be supported by the resources and practices needed to honor them. In the OPE Landscape, I describe four connected meanings of sustainability:

- **Financial sustainability:** services have identifiable funding paths, whether through institutional investment, grants, endowments, agreements, cost recovery, or other approved support. A possible funding source is a planning opportunity until support is secured.
- **Operational sustainability:** services have defined scope, workflows, staffing, and responsibilities. Growth should not depend on extraordinary effort from one person or on undocumented knowledge.
- **Technical sustainability:** platform choices support preservation, interoperability, accessibility, reliable metadata, and maintenance that the Libraries can realistically provide.
- **Intellectual sustainability:** OPE develops a research program that examines scholarly communication, open infrastructure, data publishing, open educational resources, and evidence of impact. This inquiry should help us assess and improve our services over time.

These definitions guide how I assess my progress. Writing a plan, developing a tool, and establishing an operating service are different accomplishments. Each matters, but they support different claims about what the Libraries can currently deliver. The [June OPE Landscape][landscape] brings these commitments together across publishing, repositories, open education, and data services.

Network Building is a supporting theme across this work. Relationships with colleagues, campus researchers, publishers, and professional communities help identify needs, share expertise, and develop responsibility beyond a single project or person. In 2026, those relationships supported departmental planning, faculty mentoring, student onboarding, research proposals, and national standards work.

The sections below organize these contributions around the themes discussed with Michelle and connect them to my 2026 Unclassified Performance Plan (UPP). OPE leadership and service development are the center of this account. My library research leadership complements that work by developing partnerships and inquiry that can help the Libraries understand research activity, inform collections, and guide service evolution.

## Reflection and priorities

The strongest pattern in this year’s work is the connection between service development and shared responsibility. The Landscape, faculty mentoring, student-role coordination, and external partnerships provide concrete examples of moving ideas into documents, decisions, and relationships that others can use. They also show why sustainability must be assessed at more than one level: a technically promising service still needs staff, a funding path, and a clear purpose.

I need to continue improving the transition from development to an operating service. For OPE, that means clearer ownership and workflows. For research reporting, it means validation and evidence of use. For workforce development, it means documenting what people complete and learn. For my research agenda, it means specifying the questions and partnerships that turn tools and concepts into a coherent program of inquiry.

My immediate priorities are to translate the Landscape into supportable service commitments, document the results of mentoring and training, strengthen the reliability and interpretation of research reporting, and define the next steps in the undergraduate-publishing research agenda. I also need to make this year’s library-science professional development explicit. These priorities keep the Libraries’ responsibilities to researchers, students, and colleagues at the center of my work.
