## Overview

My goal as Head of the Open Publishing Exchange (OPE) is to build scholarly communication services the Libraries can sustain. That includes how research is published, shared, preserved, and reused. I approach sustainability in four connected ways: financial support that can sustain commitments; defined workflows, staffing, and ownership; maintainable technical infrastructure; and research that helps us assess and improve services. The [June OPE Landscape][landscape] brings these commitments together across publishing, repositories, open education, and data services.

Relationships with colleagues, campus researchers, publishers, and professional communities support this work. The account below distinguishes completed contributions from proposals, prototypes, and future goals, with evidence beside the relevant examples.

## Professional Conduct

### Interact cooperatively with colleagues, show civility and respect to others with whom you work and interact, show respect for the opinions of others in the exchange of ideas, and demonstrate a willingness to follow appropriate directives from supervisors (C46.1 UH)

**Weight: High.**

I approach leadership as work done with colleagues whose expertise and ownership must remain visible. This means seeking agreement about responsibilities, giving substantive feedback, and coordinating decisions through the appropriate supervisors and administrative processes. Several documented examples show how I have put that approach into practice:

- In reviewing Gwen Sibley’s proposal drafts, I offered advice on research questions, methods, evidence, and sponsor fit while preserving her authorship and project ownership. Her replies described incorporating my recommendations.
- In preparing OPE student roles, I worked with colleagues on existing position plans, clarified primary mentors, obtained approval, and coordinated posting with Human Resources. My contribution was collaborative refinement and coordination.
- I delegated departmental representation and helped colleagues obtain training opportunities rather than treating participation and technical expertise as responsibilities that must remain with me.

These examples also show an area I intend to keep strengthening: making the transition from an idea to shared responsibility explicit. Clear ownership and timely consultation are part of both collegial practice and operational sustainability. [Supporting mentoring and staffing records][mentoring]
