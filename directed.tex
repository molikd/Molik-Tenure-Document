\section{Directed Service}

\subsection{OPE leadership}
Leading the Open Publishing Exchange (OPE), formerly the Center for Scholarly Publishing (CSP), is the center of my appointment. The department guides authors and researchers through publishing, open education, repository and research-data services, which is how the Libraries take part in creating, publishing, sharing and preserving scholarship. My leadership work is chiefly operational and financial sustainability: setting direction, aligning staffing and budget, and helping colleagues take on defined responsibilities so the department does not depend on any one person. Because OPE's services are still consolidating, many entries below are planning and coordination milestones rather than final operating outcomes, and I keep that distinction explicit.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Departmental foundation (February--December 2025).} As Department Head of CSP, I set priorities for publishing, open education and research-data services. An April 2025 presentation to research leadership and a July 2025 departmental landscape explained the direction; Tea with CSP discussions, drafting for the Open and Alternative Textbook Initiative (OATI) and oversight of publishing-platform transitions supported it.\evidenceref{P1}
\item \textbf{Explaining our services (February 2026).} I presented OPE's name and direction at the February 3 First Tuesday session and gave February 17 public-access guidance to research leaders, helping colleagues understand how the Libraries support their research.\evidenceref{D3}
\item \textbf{Writing the OPE landscape and executive summary (June 2026).} I wrote the new four-page landscape, now linked from StaffNet's department overviews. It extends the July 2025 plan across open education, repository, New Prairie Press and data services and proposes priorities for staffing, funding, technology, grant support, publishing agreements and mentoring. StaffNet links the completed document; the recommendations still require separate decisions and implementation.\evidenceref{L5}
\item \textbf{Ongoing scholarly-communication leadership analysis.} I produce continuing analysis of open publishing infrastructure, publishing agreements, repositories, persistent identifiers and data publishing to support OPE and Libraries decision-making. This is a standing leadership and planning contribution, not a one-time deliverable or an implemented service.\evidenceref{D1}
\item \textbf{Supervision, mentoring and delegation.} I helped refine and coordinate student and staff roles, assigned primary librarian mentors, coordinated Human Resources approval for three postings confirmed August 10 and welcomed three students on September 8--11. I delegated OPE representation for an Academic Services session and supported colleagues with Carpentries instructor-training access and a proposed paid open-science training program for student supervisors.\evidenceref{L2}\evidenceref{D1}
\item \textbf{Mentoring a colleague's proposal (July 2026).} I reviewed successive drafts of Gwen Sibley's William T. Grant letter of inquiry, advising on the research gap, questions, methods, funder fit, use of evidence and biosketch. Her July 22--23 replies described incorporating my recommendations, and I circulated Draft 8 on July 24; she retained authorship and ownership of the project.\evidenceref{L3}
\item \textbf{Open and Alternative Textbook Initiative governance.} I contributed governance and bylaw drafting for OATI and later represented OPE in representation, fee-scenario and student-inclusion discussions with the university body that oversees open educational resource funding and governance (BOER). These remain planning discussions; no fee, revenue or approved governance change follows automatically from them.\evidenceref{P1}
\item \textbf{Shared faculty concept for artificial-intelligence-ready data.} With colleagues I developed a concept for a shared OPE and Computer Science cluster hire supporting institutional artificial-intelligence research capacity. This is a staffing concept, not an approved position or hire.\evidenceref{P1}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Developing publishing practices}
These services help authors create, publish and share their work, and they make access to scholarship more sustainable. OPE administers the agreements, platforms and guidance that determine whether a K-State author can publish openly, deposit a manuscript or afford a fee, so this theme is where financial and technical sustainability meet the Libraries' public-access mission. The entries below distinguish services in operation from assessments and consultations still underway.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Open-access agreement administration.} I advanced OPE's administration and evaluation of open-access agreements, including work with the Association for Computing Machinery (ACM), the Royal Society of Chemistry (RSC) and the Greater Western Library Alliance (GWLA), Cambridge University Press, the Public Library of Science (PLOS) and PeerJ. These are named workstreams; original agreements, renewals and completed negotiations still require their own evidence, and no author-savings figure is claimed from the inventory alone.\evidenceref{D1}
\item \textbf{Textbook 2.0 fundraising (April 8, 2026).} I restarted the K-State Textbook 2.0 campaign, which raised \$1,920 against \$385 in 2025. I report these as fundraising, not student savings; receipts and attribution remain to be retained.\evidenceref{D2}
\item \textbf{Open-access and copyright consultation (August 26, 2026).} I identified a possible Green open-access route to make two forthcoming book chapters available by depositing permitted manuscript versions in a repository. I explained K-State publishing agreements, OPE services, New Prairie Press, K-REx, OATI and developing data publishing. Any avoided publication charges -- a potential \$7,400 -- remain potential savings, not realized ones.\evidenceref{D2}
\item \textbf{Understanding Writing textbook and final report (June 24, 2026).} OATI program activity culminated in a completed open textbook, \textit{Understanding Writing}, and its final grant report, routed to OATI reviewers. This is a completed program outcome; my particular oversight contribution remains to be documented more fully, and student impact is a separate measure.\evidenceref{P1}
\item \textbf{Public-access guidance and outreach (February 17, 2026).} I presented public-access guidance to research leadership, and my June 17 Research Weekly article, ``What is the Serials Crisis?'', explained why publishing costs matter for access. Leadership confirmed publication and passed along favorable feedback; the article is published outreach, while a later open-data and open-access draft does not establish a second published piece.\evidenceref{D3}\evidenceref{D1}
\item \textbf{Data-publishing program direction.} I developed OPE's data-publishing direction, extending the department beyond journals, monographs, OER, repository and open-access administration toward managed research-data publishing, and explained authors' guidance and publishing requirements. The technical platform work belongs under Data and infrastructure development.\evidenceref{D1}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Data and infrastructure development}
This theme covers the repositories, platforms and tools that keep scholarship findable, connected and preserved. Technical choices here determine whether content survives, whether identifiers resolve, whether metadata is usable and whether maintenance is realistic -- the technical dimension of sustainability. My contributions are often coordination, policy or software design within larger institutional efforts, so I identify what I did separately from outcomes owned by the institution or by collaborators.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Research-data consultation (April 16, 2026).} I provided research-data consultation for Jared Crain involving approximately 250 terabytes of data. This reports the scale of a consultation, not a completed deposit, volume curated or preservation commitment.\evidenceref{D2}
\item \textbf{K-REx repository policy and content model.} I advanced K-REx repository policy for Crossref integration and collection-level metadata defaults with item overrides, and expanded the content model with new types including Research, Scholarly and Creative Activity (RSCAD), open-access manuscripts, reports, working papers, standards and educational resources. Original tickets and configuration records are still needed to establish which changes were implemented and my exact role.\evidenceref{D1}
\item \textbf{DSpace 9.3 upgrade and repository connections (September 2, 2026 onward).} After the reported DSpace 9.3 upgrade, I coordinated discussion of connections with ORCID, OpenAIRE and Crossref alongside preservation and reporting tools, to make deposited work easier to find, connect and preserve. My contribution was coordination; the integrations themselves are proposed, not completed.\evidenceref{D1}
\item \textbf{ORCID guidance (September 2, 2026).} Carol Sevin confirmed adding my suggestion about ORCID researcher identifiers to her research guidance resources. This is confirmed incorporation of a suggestion; fuller explanatory material was still planned, and it is distinct from the grant consultation above.\evidenceref{L4}
\item \textbf{InvenioRDM repository pilot.} I advanced a pilot of the InvenioRDM data repository on K-State infrastructure, incorporating concepts such as Amazon Web Services storage, Ceph and the InterPlanetary File System, ORCID and planned DataCite digital object identifier minting. \item \textbf{Figshare procurement and sustainability model.} I assessed Figshare procurement and a data-publishing sustainability model, weighing storage, pricing, acceptance criteria and possible data-note or external-publication requirements. This is assessment and planning; no purchase or implemented service is assumed.\evidenceref{D1}
\item \textbf{Preservation and usage infrastructure.} I advanced work with Controlled LOCKSS (CLOCKSS), a shared digital-preservation archive, and the Institutional Repository Usage Statistics (IRUS) service. \item \textbf{Fungal Genetics Reports platform transition.} I supported the transition of \textit{Fungal Genetics Reports} toward the Janeway publishing platform as part of publishing-platform modernization; the inventory's completion wording still needs a completion record. Editorial leadership of the journal belongs under Research, Scholarly and Creative Activity.\evidenceref{P1}
\item \textbf{Pressbooks--Canvas integration assessment.} I assessed the current Pressbooks--Canvas integration, hosting and institutional use for OER delivery, along with renewal analysis. The analysis and any renewal decision remain to be retained before an integration or renewal is claimed.\evidenceref{D1}

This existing creative artifact is preserved here; its date, my contribution and use still need confirmation before stronger impact claims.\evidenceref{S1}
\item \textbf{Earlier data and campus infrastructure planning.} Earlier research-data management and curation work, research-information-system planning and campus infrastructure discussions remain the historical foundation of this theme. Those planning efforts do not by themselves establish full deployment.\evidenceref{P1}
