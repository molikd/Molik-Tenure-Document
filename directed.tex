\section{Directed Service}

\subsection{OPE leadership}
Leading the Open Publishing Exchange (OPE), formerly the Center for Scholarly Publishing (CSP), is the center of my appointment/position. The department guides authors and researchers through publishing, open education, repository and research-data services, which is how the Libraries take part in creating, publishing, sharing  scholarship. My leadership work is chiefly operational and financial sustainability: setting direction, aligning staffing and budget, and helping colleagues take on defined responsibilities so the department does not depend on any one person. Because OPE's services are still consolidating, many entries below are planning and coordination milestones rather than final operating outcomes, and I keep that distinction explicit. Together, the entries trace a full arc across the review period: I set the department's direction in 2025, explained it again across the Libraries in early 2026, and converted it into a written landscape, defined roles, and mentored staff by September.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Departmental foundation (February--December 2025).} As Department Head of CSP, I set priorities for publishing, open education and research-data services. An April 2025 presentation to research leadership and a July 2025 departmental landscape explained the direction; Tea with CSP discussions (now OPEnhouse), drafting for the Open and Alternative Textbook Initiative (OATI) and oversight of publishing-platform transitions supported it.\evidenceref[Prior portfolio and appointment records]{P1}

It reorganizes the July 2025 plan across open education, repository, New Prairie Press and data services and proposes priorities for staffing, funding, technology, grant support, publishing agreements and mentoring. StaffNet links the completed document; the recommendations still require separate decisions and implementation.\evidenceref[OPE landscape and executive summary]{L5} A yearly landscape document is now OPE Department Head practice.

Whlie Collection Services establishes these agreements, OPE administers the agreements, platforms and guidance that determine whether a K-State author can publish openly, deposit a manuscript or afford a fee, so this theme is where financial and technical sustainability meet the Libraries' public-access mission. The entries below distinguish services in operation from assessments and consultations still underway. In additon OPE operates the Institional Repository, and New Prairie Press, and Open Educational Resoruces which require defining stratagey in these areas. \subsection{Data and infrastructure development}
This theme covers the repositories, platforms and tools that keep scholarship findable, connected and preserved. Technical choices here determine whether content survives, whether identifiers resolve, whether metadata is usable and whether maintenance is realistic -- the technical dimension of sustainability. My contributions are often coordination, policy or software design within larger institutional efforts, so I identify what I did separately from outcomes owned by the institution or by collaborators. Taken together, the entries show parallel tracks -- repository policy, platform pilots, preservation, and reporting tools -- coordinated toward scholarship that is findable, connected, and preserved.

\begin{itemize}
