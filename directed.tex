\section{Leading publishing, education and research services}
\textbf{February--December 2025: departmental foundation.} As Department Head of the Center for Scholarly Publishing, now the Open Publishing Exchange (OPE), I worked on priorities for publishing, open education and research-data services. My April presentation to research leadership and July departmental landscape explained this direction. Tea with CSP discussions, drafting for the Open and Alternative Textbook Initiative and oversight of publishing-platform transitions supported the work. I also undertook leadership and assessment development through Harvard’s Leadership Institute for Academic Librarians, the New Department Head Institute and Assessment in Action.\evidenceref{P1}

\textbf{February 2026: explaining our services.} My achievements record identifies a February 3 First Tuesday presentation on OPE's name and direction and February 17 guidance to research leaders on public access. These continued the work of helping colleagues understand how the Libraries can support their research.\evidenceref{D3}

\textbf{March 17, 2026 onward: planning data support for a grant.} Following Cassandra Olds's request, I helped define OPE's data responsibilities for the SAS proposal concept. The proposed five-year OPE budget was \$448,798, with me providing initial technical leadership and Gwen Sibley taking on day-to-day research-data management. This addressed costs and responsibilities early in project planning; the amount is a proposed component, not the whole grant or an award.\evidenceref{G1}

\textbf{April 2026: open education and data consultation.} The April 8 achievement record reports Textbook 2.0 receipts of \$1,920, compared with \$385 in 2025. The April 16 record describes a consultation involving approximately 250 TB of research data. These describe funds raised and the scale of a consultation; they do not measure student savings or data deposited.\evidenceref{D2}

\textbf{May 1, 2026: Lead Research Officer agreement.} Correspondence confirms that my agreement was signed, and August 5 Strategic Leadership Council minutes also identify the role. My responsibilities include developing proposals and partnerships, defining project work, writing and budgeting. This gives me a formal role in helping researchers involve the Libraries early in their projects.\evidenceref{L1}

\textbf{May 2026: helping workshop participants continue their work.} After the data-curation workshops, I sent participants publishing, repository, evidence-synthesis and AgBioData resources on May 29. A participant replied positively to this support. Individual teaching hours and attendance totals have not been established.\evidenceref{D1}

\textbf{June 1, 2026 onward: research-publication reporting.} The Office of the Vice President for Research asked for help understanding K-State's publication activity. I worked on how reports should handle citation periods, author affiliations, source quality, open access and outputs beyond journal articles. I coordinated Libraries participation and developed three related approaches: HAIL collects and organizes records while retaining their sources; HAIL-local produces reports from locally stored data; and Prairie Observatory provides dashboard views. Together, these tools help colleagues examine research activity and the limits of the available data.\evidenceref{K1}

\textbf{June 2026: planning OPE's next steps.} I wrote the new OPE landscape and executive summary, extending the July 2025 plan across open education, repositories, New Prairie Press and data services. It proposes priorities for staffing, funding, technology, grant support, publishing agreements and mentoring. StaffNet links the completed document among its department overviews; the proposals still require separate decisions and implementation.\evidenceref{L5}

\textbf{June 17, 2026: explaining publishing costs.} My Research Weekly article, ``What is the Serials Crisis?'', explained why publishing costs matter for access to scholarship. Libraries leadership confirmed publication and passed along favorable feedback, continuing my scholarly-communication outreach from 2025.\evidenceref{D1}

\textbf{July 10--24, 2026: mentoring a colleague's proposal.} I reviewed several drafts of Gwen Sibley's William T. Grant letter of inquiry. My advice addressed the research gap, questions, methods, fit with the funder, use of evidence and biosketch. Her July 22--23 replies described incorporating my recommendations, and I circulated Draft 8 on July 24. Gwen retained authorship and ownership of the project.\evidenceref{L3}

\textbf{July 21, 2026 onward: helping develop student roles.} I helped colleagues refine planned positions in data and digital scholarship, publishing and repositories, and open educational resources and accessibility. I suggested technical work, primary librarian mentors and opportunities for increasing responsibility, revised descriptions and coordinated approval with Human Resources. Three postings were confirmed August 10; September 8--11 records document welcoming three students.\evidenceref{L2}

\textbf{August 4--17, 2026: sharing responsibilities and preparing training.} I delegated OPE representation for an Academic Services session, helped colleagues obtain Carpentries instructor-training access and proposed paid initial open-science training for student supervisors. These steps supported colleagues in taking on work; training completion and learning outcomes remain to be documented.\evidenceref{L2}\evidenceref{D1}

\textbf{August 11, 2026 onward: Sunderland planning.} After a discussion with Jeff Sheldon, I delivered an operating-model workbook to Jeff, Tara Coleman and James Wiser on August 12. It distinguished routine services from advanced technical and research work and considered Advanced Technological Education (ATE) and Improving Undergraduate STEM Education (IUSE) funding opportunities. Jeff responded positively to the write-up. By September 11, we had deferred ATE to the following year to strengthen the partnership with MATC and other collaborators. The workbook is a completed planning contribution; the program is not funded.\evidenceref{L4}

\textbf{August 26, 2026: helping authors consider open access.} My achievements record identifies a possible route to make two book chapters available by depositing permitted manuscript versions in a repository. Any avoided publication charges remain potential savings.\evidenceref{D2}

\textbf{August 27--September 2026: reviewing preliminary reports.} Hans Coetzee praised progress on August 27, and Lowell Scott gave positive feedback on the report's appearance in September. The reports still need validation and consistent updates before their figures can be used as final institutional measures. Evidence of their use in decisions is also pending. Namratha Kommineni contributed the Power BI prototype.\evidenceref{K1}

\textbf{September 1--2, 2026: advising on grants and research guidance.} I met with Carol Sevin about grant development and shared opportunities from the U.S. Department of Agriculture and National Science Foundation. Separately, Carol confirmed adding my suggestion about ORCID researcher identifiers to research guidance.\evidenceref{L4}

\textbf{September 2, 2026 onward: connecting repository services.} After the reported DSpace 9.3 upgrade, I coordinated discussion of connections with ORCID, OpenAIRE and Crossref, alongside preservation and reporting tools. The purpose was to make deposited work easier to find, connect and preserve. My contribution was coordination; implementation of these proposed connections remains separate.\evidenceref{D1}

\textbf{September 11--14, 2026: preparing an IUSE proposal.} I identified the opportunity, developed a question comparing maker-based approaches to science, technology, engineering and mathematics (STEM) learning, recruited collaborators including Melia Fritch and shared an example with the team. The September 16 meeting falls after this draft's cutoff.\evidenceref{L4}

\textbf{September 14--15, 2026: proposing OAK-AI services.} I proposed shared research-data and publishing support for the OAK-AI Hub discussion. These are proposals for the team's consideration, not agreed responsibilities or services already in operation.\evidenceref{G2}
