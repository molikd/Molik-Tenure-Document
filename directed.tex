\section{Cumulative leadership and service development}
\textbf{February--December 2025: departmental foundation.} As CSP/OPE Department Head, I worked on service priorities across publishing, open education and research-data stewardship. I presented the department's direction to campus research leadership in April and prepared the departmental landscape and executive summary in July. Tea with CSP, OATI governance drafting, and oversight of publishing-platform transition work supported planning and professional exchange. My leadership/assessment development included LIAL, the New Department Head Institute and Assessment in Action. The 2026 follow-through below emphasizes attributable actions and outcomes; implementation of every earlier plan is not assumed.\evidenceref{P1}

\textbf{February 2026: communicating the service direction.} The achievements record identifies my February 3 First Tuesday presentation on OPE's name and program direction and February 17 public-access guidance for research leadership. These continue the 2025 work of explaining how Libraries services connect to research needs.\evidenceref{D3}

\textbf{March 17, 2026 onward: research-service scoping.} Following Cassandra Olds's request, I contributed an OPE data-infrastructure scope for the SAS concept, with my initial technical leadership transitioning toward Gwen Sibley's operational RDM leadership. This puts data stewardship into project planning, where its costs and responsibilities can be addressed early. The proposed five-year OPE component totals \$448,798; it is neither the whole proposal nor an award.\evidenceref{G1}

\textbf{April 2026: open education and data consultation.} The April 8 achievement record reports Textbook 2.0 receipts of \$1,920, compared with \$385 in 2025. The April 16 record describes a consultation involving approximately 250 TB of research data. These measures describe fundraising and consultation scale, not student savings or a completed deposit. They extend OPE's work to lower barriers to learning materials and help researchers manage data for reuse.\evidenceref{D2}

\textbf{May 1, 2026: research leadership agreement.} Administrative correspondence confirms execution of my Lead Research Officer MOU; August 5 SLC minutes corroborate the role. The agreement describes proposal and partnership development, collaborative funding, project scoping, writing and budgeting. These responsibilities put the Libraries' strategic priority of advancing the research lifecycle into practice through early partnership in research development.\evidenceref{L1}

\textbf{May 2026: participant support.} Following the May data-curation workshops, I sent participants publishing, repository, evidence-synthesis and AgBioData resources on May 29. A participant's positive reply supplies qualitative feedback on that support. The records do not establish my individual teaching hours or attendance totals.\evidenceref{D1}

\textbf{June 1, 2026 onward: research-publication reporting.} In response to OVPR, I addressed citation windows, affiliations, source quality, open-access categories and outputs beyond articles. I coordinated Libraries participation and developed related collection and reporting approaches: HAIL's normalized, provenance-aware data layer, HAIL-local's cached reports and lightweight Prairie Observatory views. These related approaches serve OPE's evidence agenda: help colleagues interpret what research measures can and cannot tell them.\evidenceref{K1}

\textbf{June 2026: departmental landscape and sustainability planning.} I prepared the new OPE landscape and executive summary, extending the July 2025 plan. It connects the four service lines through sustainable funding, staffing, technology and research, with priorities for grant support, publishing agreements, evidence and mentoring. StaffNet links it among the department overviews. The writing is complete; proposed staffing, funding and services remain distinct from approved or implemented outcomes.\evidenceref{L5}

\textbf{June 17, 2026: published outreach.} My Research Weekly article, ``What is the Serials Crisis?'', translated publishing conditions for the campus community to explain why sustainable alternatives matter for access to scholarship. Leadership correspondence confirms publication and favorable qualitative response, extending the previous year's scholarly-communication outreach.\evidenceref{D1}
